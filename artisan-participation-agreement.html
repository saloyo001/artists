ARTISAN PARTICIPATION AGREEMENT About this agreement This document is an agreement prepared for a Kenyan online marketplace in this case "Mzalendo" that connects Kenyan buyers with Kenyan artisans selling handmade products or creative services. The platform is at a pilot stage and hence it currently operates through informal founder arrangements except after acknowledgment of this contract, communicates with buyers and artisans through [phone calls, in person visit, WhatsApp chats and social media direct messages or official channel], and does not yet process live payments. This contract is written in accordance with the laws of Kenya, and such shall operate within its auspices. Status markers used in this document The platform operates in a regulated legal environment, and different statements in this document carry different kinds of meaning. To keep the three apart, some paragraphs are marked as follows: • LEGAL REQUIREMENT: a statement of a requirement or risk that arises under Kenyan law. These statements describe obligations or prohibitions that apply regardless of what this agreement says, and the agreement cannot override them. • RECOMMENDED PRACTICE: a step or safeguard that is not compelled by statute but is strongly advisable, for example because it reduces legal, financial or reputational risk. • BUSINESS POLICY: a choice the founders have made or must make about how the platform will operate. These are decisions for the operator, not legal conclusions, and they may be changed by the operator through the amendment process in clause 12. Where a paragraph carries no marker, it is either ordinary contract text or a statement that applies at the level of this agreement rather than at the level of statute. 1. PURPOSE AND PILOT STATUS 3.1 Purpose of the Agreement The purpose of this Agreement is to set fair, clear terms under which Kenyan artisans can participate in the platform during its pilot stage, covering verification of artisans and listings, intellectual property, fair competition, data protection, content security, withdrawal of listings, orders and payments, complaints and dispute resolution, and suspension and termination. 3.2 Voluntary participation Joining the platform is entirely voluntary. No artisan is required to join, and an artisan who does not join loses nothing to which they were otherwise entitled. An artisan may decline to accept this Agreement, may decline individual features, and may withdraw under clause 9 at any time. 3.3 No guarantee of sales or income The Operators do not promise any particular level of exposure, sales, orders or income although it is the intended goal of the platform. Listing a product does not guarantee that any buyer will see it, enquire about it, or purchase it. Any figures, projections or examples of sales shown on the platform or in communications are illustrations only and are not commitments, forecasts or advice. 3.4 No commissions during the Pilot Period During the Pilot Period the Operators will not charge any commission, fee or percentage on transactions between buyers and Artisans, and will not receive, hold or redistribute buyer funds, as further described in clause 10. If a fee of any kind is introduced later, clause 3.5 applies. 3.5 Future commissions and fees In the event the Operators ever intends to charge a commission, listing fee, subscription, transaction fee or any other charge, the Operator must, before the charge takes effect: • disclose the exact calculation method, including the base amount, the percentage or fixed amount, and who bears payment provider fees; • disclose the effective date from which the charge applies; • disclose how the charge will be invoiced, paid, reconciled and receipted; and • obtain the Artisan's express, informed agreement to the charge, in accordance with clause 12.6 where the change is material. No commission or fee applies to any transaction completed before the disclosed effective date of the charge. The Operators will not apply any commission retrospectively to transactions completed before the charge was disclosed and agreed. 3.6 End of the Pilot Period The Operator will give Artisans at least 14 days written and oral notice before the Pilot Period ends or before material changes to how the platform operates take effect, in accordance with clause 12.6. With the transparent notification, The Operators intends that, at the end of the pilot, Artisans will be given a genuine choice whether to continue on the then-current terms. 3.7 Simulated payments distinguished from real payments During the Pilot Period, any payment flow demonstrated or tested on or through the platform, including any simulated checkout, is a Simulated Payment and does not transfer real money. The Operators will clearly label simulated flows as simulations at the point of use. Real payments, when they exist, will be made directly between buyers and Artisans or through a Payment Provider as described in clause 10, and the Operator will announce the change clearly before it happens. An Artisan must never treat a simulated confirmation screen, notification or screenshot as proof that money has been received. 4. APPLICATION AND VERIFICATION 4.1 Purpose of verification The verification process exists to give buyers a fair basis for trust and to protect honest artisans from impersonation and misrepresentation, while remaining proportionate to a pilot-stage platform. 4.2 Application process An applicant submits an application through a WhatsApp contact number that shall be provided or an online form containing: • full legal name and contact details; • identity confirmation, being a photograph or scan of a Kenyan national identity card or passport, held and used only for the purposes in clause 7; • a description of the products the applicant wishes to list, including materials, production methods, and the origin of materials or designs where known; • evidence of authorship of, or involvement in making, the products, which may include photographs or videos of the making process, the workshop or workspace, tools, work in progress, or the applicant working on the products; and • any further information reasonably needed to assess the application under clause 4.5. 4.3 Proportionality The level of verification requested will be proportionate to the risk. For example, an artisan selling items they personally make from their own workshop may not be required to produce the same evidence as a person selling items made by a large collective. The Operators may apply lighter requirements to low-volume applicants and heavier requirements where volumes, prices or the use of collaborators increase risk. This will not be on the basis of discrimination of any kind. 4.4 Evidence of authorship and making Evidence of making may include, in any combination: photographs or videos showing the making process; images of the workspace or tools; progressive images of a work in progress; descriptions of technique and materials; and, where the applicant chooses, references from customers or community members. The Operators will accept a range of evidence types and will not require a single specific form of proof where a combination of evidence is reasonably capable of establishing authorship or involvement. 4.5 Selling products made by others The platform is open not only to artisans who personally make everything they sell, but also to Collaborative Products and to authorised sellers of other makers' work, provided the relationship is stated honestly. An applicant who lists products made, in whole or in part, by a cooperative, an employee, a family member, another artisan or any third party must: • state clearly, in the application and in each Listing, who made the product and the applicant's own role; • provide evidence of authorisation to sell, such as a written agreement, a letter from the cooperative, or a documented customary arrangement acceptable to the Operator; and • comply with the Labelling Categories in clause 4.6. 4.6 Labelling categories Listings must carry one of the following honest labels: • "Made by me": made personally by the Artisan. • "Made by my collective/team": made by the Artisan together with employees, family members or fellow members of a group the Artisan belongs to or controls, named or described in the Listing. • "Made by [named maker/collective], sold with authorisation": made by a third party, with the Artisan acting as Authorised Representative, with the maker identified to the extent the maker agrees. • "Resold": lawfully resold goods not made by the Artisan, with any available maker or provenance information stated honestly. A Reseller is not presumed dishonest. Selling the work of others with authorisation and honest labelling is a legitimate business. Mislabelling a category, however, is a verification and fair-competition issue under clauses 4.10 and 5. 4.7 Verification assessment The Operators will assess applications fairly and consistently, against the published criteria, and will decide within a reasonable period, ordinarily 5 to 10 Business Days or less or more, subjectively. The Operator may ask clarifying questions and reasonable supplementary evidence before deciding. 4.8 Rejection and suspension of applications The Operators may reject an application, or suspend a pending application, where: the identity cannot reasonably be confirmed; the evidence of making or authorisation is materially insufficient; there are reasonable grounds to believe the application involves impersonation, forged or falsified evidence, misleading claims about origin or authorship, or other dishonesty; or the applicant breaches this Agreement during application. The Operators will give written reasons for a rejection or suspension under clause 4.10. 4.9 Verification badges and their exact meaning A Verification Badge on a Listing or profile means only that: the applicant submitted the identity and authorship evidence described in clause 4.2, or the authorisation evidence described in clause 4.5; and the Operators reviewed that evidence and considered it, on balance, to be consistent with the applicant's stated role. The Badge is a limited administrative check performed by a small pilot-stage team. It is not a guarantee that the products are authentic, that claims in a Listing are true, that any particular transaction will be completed, or that the Operators have investigated any matter beyond the verification materials. The Badge does not certify quality, provenance, legality, or origin of materials. The platform will state this limited meaning wherever the Badge is explained to buyers. 4.10 Decisions, clarification and challenge Where the Operators takes an adverse verification decision, including rejection, suspension, downgrade or withdrawal of a Verification Badge, the Operators will: • give the affected person written notice of the decision and the reasons for it; • give a reasonable opportunity, ordinarily at least 5 Business Days, to clarify, correct or challenge the decision with further evidence; and • have the review conducted, so far as practicable in a small team, by a person not involved in the original decision, in accordance with clause 11. A decision may be maintained, changed or reversed after review. Reasons will be given for the outcome. 4.11 Fraudulent applications Providing false identity documents, forged authorisation letters, impersonation of another maker, or materially false statements in an application is dishonest and may also constitute criminal conduct under Kenyan law, including provisions on forgery and fraud. The Operators may reject the application, refuse future applications, and report conduct it reasonably believes is criminal to relevant authorities. Where a fraudulent application involves misuse of another person's identity or work, the Operator will also consider the IP provisions of clause 6 and the complaint process of clause 11. 4.12 Records of verification Verification materials are personal data and are handled under clause 7 and the Privacy Notice, including access restriction, retention limits and secure deletion. Original identity documents are used for confirmation and are not published on the platform. 5. FAIR COMPETITION AND CONDUCT 5.1 Honest descriptions and pricing Each Artisan must describe their products honestly: materials, dimensions, production method, origin, and the maker's identity and role as required by clause 4.6. Prices must reflect the Artisan's genuine intended terms, and must not be presented in a way that misleads buyers about total cost, delivery or the nature of the product. 5.2 False claims of originality or authenticity No Artisan may claim, expressly or by implication, that a product is handmade, original, traditional, authentic, made by a particular community, or of particular materials, where that claim is false or misleading. A claim of cultural origin, for example that a product is made in a particular community or technique, must be accurate and, where applicable, made only with the involvement or authorisation the claim implies. 5.3 Impersonation and harassment No Artisan may impersonate another artisan, buyer, or the Operators; harass, threaten, abuse or discriminate against any person on the platform; or engage in conduct intended to damage another artisan's reputation. 5.4 No interference with other artisans' businesses No Artisan may deliberately interfere with another artisan's business on the platform, including through false reports, fake orders intended to waste another artisan's time or cost, manipulation of reviews or feedback, or misleading communications directed at another artisan's customers. 5.5 Confidential business information No Artisan may misuse Confidential Information of the Operators or of other artisans, including unpublished designs, supplier or buyer lists, pricing strategies and verification materials, except as permitted by clause 6.11 and this clause 5.5. 5.6 Competition law boundaries Under section 21 of the Competition Act, 2010 (Act No. 12 of 2010), agreements between competitors to fix prices, rig bids, divide markets or share markets or sources of supply are restrictive trade practices that are prohibited. Artisans on the platform are independent businesses. Accordingly: Artisans must not agree with other artisans on prices, on dividing customers, locations or product types, or on coordinated refusals to deal; and the Operators will not organise or facilitate any such agreement. Each Artisan sets their own prices independently, and each remains free to compete lawfully for buyers, including by competing on price, quality, design and service. Nothing in this Agreement, in platform communications, or in any artisan group chat operated by the Operator should be read as recommending, coordinating or facilitating price alignment or any other conduct described in clause 5.6. The Operator will avoid forum features that invite coordinated pricing, and will state this boundary where artisans discuss business together. 5.7 Copying protected work versus independent creation Copying protected work is dealt with under clause 6. Independently creating a product that resembles another artisan's product, without copying protected expression such as protected designs, protected works or registered marks, is not of itself a breach of this Agreement and is part of ordinary competition. Artisans should not use complaint processes under clause 11 to suppress independent competition; false or malicious complaints are addressed in clauses 6.13 and 11.12. 5.8 Why artisan-protection rules must not become restraints of trade The protections in this Agreement, including verification, IP enforcement and honest labelling, exist to prevent deception and to protect rights holders. They must not be used to create unlawful restraints on trade, such as rules that fix prices, allocate markets, restrict an artisan from selling through other channels, or prevent lawful independent competition. Any provision of this Agreement, or any platform practice, that would operate as such a restraint is to that extent contrary to this clause and to the Competition Act, 2010, and may be raised through clause 11 or with the Competition Authority of Kenya. 6. INTELLECTUAL PROPERTY 6.1 Ownership by the Artisan All IP Rights in an Artisan's original designs, products, photographs, descriptions, logos and branding remain the property of the Artisan or of their lawful licensor. Nothing in this Agreement transfers ownership of any of those rights to the Operator. This clause states the default position; it does not determine ownership where the law places it elsewhere, including under clauses 6.4 to 6.7. 6.2 Ownership of a product versus IP in its design Ownership of a physical product is separate from ownership of IP in its design. A buyer who purchases a physical product generally owns that physical item, but does not, by reason of purchase, acquire the copyright or design rights in its design or the right to reproduce the design commercially. Conversely, an Artisan who sells a product retains IP Rights in the design unless those rights were assigned by written agreement. This distinction governs how buyer requests, reseller arrangements and image use are handled on the platform. 6.3 Licence to the Operator The Artisan grants the Operators a limited, non-exclusive, non-transferable, revocable licence to use the Listing Content solely to: display the Listing on the platform; promote the Listing and the platform, including on the platform's own social media channels; and operate the platform, including backups and moderation. The licence does not permit the Operators to sell Listing Content, to license it to third parties for the third parties' own use, to manufacture products from the designs, or to use Listing Content after termination in any new promotion. The licence terminates automatically, and the Operators' rights to continue displaying the Listing Content ends, when the Artisan withdraws or the Listing is removed under clause 9, subject only to clause 9.6 and to technical propagation delays in removing cached content. The Operators may retain copies in backups solely for operational and legal purposes for the backup retention period in clause 9.6. 6.4 Third-party photographs An Artisan may use photographs taken by another person, including a professional photographer, only where the Artisan has permission to use them for the platform, and must state the photographer's role if the Listing claims authorship of the photographs. The Artisan must not use another artisan's photographs of similar products as if they depicted the Artisan's own products. 6.5 Commissioned designs Where a design was created for the Artisan by a designer under a commission, ownership of IP depends on the terms of that commission, because under Kenyan law the author of a work is generally the first owner of copyright, subject to agreement to the contrary. The Artisan must state honestly who designed the product where the design is by another person, and must not list commissioned designs the Artisan is not licensed to sell or reproduce. 6.6 Employee-created work Where an employee of the Artisan creates a work in the course of employment, the employer is generally the first owner of copyright in the work under the Copyright Act, 2001 (Act No. 12 of 2001) (Chapter 130, Laws of Kenya). An Artisan who operates with employees should state their role under clause 4.6 and is responsible for ensuring that the employment relationship and ownership position are honestly presented. 6.7 Joint works and collaborative creations Where a product or design is created jointly, the rights of the joint authors or makers depend on their agreement and on the law. Artisans listing Collaborative Products should, where practicable, record among the makers who owns what, and must present the collaboration honestly in the Listing. The Operator does not decide disputes between makers about their shares; those disputes may be raised under clause 11 as between the makers, or in court. 6.8 Prohibited conduct No Artisan may: copy, reproduce or commercially exploit another artisan's or any third party's protected work without permission or a lawful exception; impersonate another maker or trader; remove or alter rights-management information or attribution from another's work; or use another artisan's name, mark, photographs or brand in a way that is likely to confuse buyers or to suggest false endorsement. 6.9 Reporting suspected infringement A rights holder or a person authorised to act for them may report suspected infringement through a preferred complaints channel, stating the work concerned, where it appears on the platform, the rights claimed and the basis of the claim, with supporting evidence. Reports are handled under clause 11. 6.10 Evidence and preservation The parties to an IP report should preserve relevant evidence, including original design files, making-process records, dated photographs, prior publications and communications. During an investigation under clause 11, the Operators may request such evidence, will handle it as confidential, and will retain it while needed for the investigation and any resulting review. 6.11 Temporary restrictions during investigation On receiving a report that raises, on its face, a credible question of infringement, the Operators may temporarily restrict a Listing's visibility pending investigation. Restrictions are temporary, are proportionate to the concern, and are lifted promptly where the report is not substantiated or the matter is resolved. A restriction pending investigation is not a finding of infringement and does not of itself imply wrongdoing. This clause does not limit any lawful power of the Operators to remove content that is manifestly unlawful. 6.12 Counter-notifications and response A person whose Listing is restricted or removed under clause 6.11 may submit a counter-notification stating why they believe the use is lawful, with supporting evidence. The complainant has an opportunity to respond. The Operators will review both accounts and give a written outcome with reasons under clause 11.9. 6.13 Repeat infringement and sanctions; false complaints Sanctions for infringement are proportionate and graduated, and may include correction or labelling requirements, listing restrictions, suspension under clause 12.1 or termination under clause 12.2, taking into account the scale, intent and recurrence of the conduct. A person who files a report knowing it to be false or malicious, or who uses the complaint process to suppress lawful competition as described in clause 5.7, may themselves be subject to graduated sanctions, and this does not limit any legal remedy available to the person complained against. 6.14 Confidential and unpublished designs Artisans may submit designs to the Operators for review or planned launch before public release. The Operators will treat unpublished designs as Confidential Information, will restrict access to the persons who need it for the stated purpose, will not publish them without consent, and will secure them in accordance with clause 8. This duty is subject to the practical limits described in clause 8.5. 6.15 Removal requests An Artisan may request removal of their own content at any time under clause 9. Where content is lawfully used by another artisan under an authorisation, a removal request by the rights holder is handled under clause 6.11 and clause 11, balancing the rights holder's request against the authorised user's documented rights; the authorised user's evidence of authorisation under clause 4.5 is relevant to this assessment. 6.16 The real limits of IP protection This clause states accurately what Kenyan law does and does not protect, so that artisans can make informed decisions and so that the platform does not over-promise. • Copyright protects original works of authorship fixed in a tangible form, including literary, artistic and musical works, under the Copyright Act, 2001 (Chapter 130). It protects the particular expression of an idea, not the idea itself. General techniques, methods, styles, concepts, common product shapes dictated by function, and ordinary geometric forms are generally not protected by copyright, and others may lawfully make products of a similar kind or in a similar general style if they do not copy protected expression. Protection is not conditional on registration, though registration of copyright with the Kenya Copyright Board is available and can assist enforcement. • Industrial designs can be registered at the Kenya Industrial Property Institute (KIPI) under the Industrial Property Act, 2001 (Act No. 3 of 2001). Registration gives rights in the ornamental or aesthetic aspects of an article as registered, and an unregistered industrial design does not obtain the statutory protection that registration gives. Registration is a deliberate step the Artisan must decide to take and maintain. • Trade marks are governed by the Trade Marks Act (Chapter 506, Laws of Kenya). A registered trade mark gives statutory rights and an infringement action. An unregistered mark gives no statutory action for infringement as such, although passing off remedies may be available in respect of unregistered marks at common law. Registering a brand name or logo is a step the Artisan must take to obtain the statutory protection. • Confidentiality can protect valuable undisclosed information, including designs not yet published, through the law of confidence and, where applicable, clause 6.11. Once a design is published without protection, confidentiality cannot restore it. • Traditional knowledge and cultural expressions have special protection under the Protection of Traditional Knowledge and Cultural Expressions Act, 2016 (Act No. 15 of 2016). Communities' traditional knowledge and cultural expressions are protected by that Act; an artisan who draws on them should be accurate about origin and authorisation, and a community's rights are not displaced by individual claims. The Operator will not promise that copying can be eliminated. The protections available are those described in this clause, enforced through the complaint process in clause 11, and they are complemented, not replaced, by the technical measures in clause 8 and by the artisan's own decisions to register designs, marks or copyright where they choose to. 7. DATA PROTECTION 7.1 Roles Under the Data Protection Act, 2019, a "data controller" determines the purposes and means of processing personal data, and a "data processor" processes personal data on behalf of a controller. In practice: the Operators determines why and how artisan data such as identity documents, contact details and verification records are collected and used, and is therefore a data controller in respect of that data. Where the Operators merely follows another party's instructions regarding data of which that party determines the purposes, the Operators acts as a processor for that party. Third-party services used by the platform, such as hosting providers, WhatsApp, payment processors and couriers, are controllers of their own processing and processors or controllers in relation to the platform's data depending on the service and the contractual terms. The precise allocation of roles for any given dataset should be recorded by the founders with legal advice; this clause states the framework and does not fix every label in advance. 7.2 Categories, purposes and lawful bases The platform collects and processes the following personal data. Each category lists its purpose and, as required by the Data Protection Act, the lawful basis relied on. The Operators' current lawful-basis choices are stated here and may be revisited with legal advice as the platform evolves. • Identity details and contact information (name, ID or passport, telephone, email address): verification under clause 4; communication; and performance of this Agreement at application; and legal compliance where verification is necessary to provide the service. Lawful basis: consent or performance of this Agreement, as applicable. • Verification notes and decisions: assessment of applications; handling appeals and complaints; operation of the verification framework. Lawful basis: performance of this Agreement and legitimate interests; defending legal claims. • Product and Listing information: display and operation of Listings; promotion as licensed under clause 6.3. Lawful basis: performance of this Agreement; consent where the Artisan opts into promotion features. • Order records and communications with buyers through platform channels: recording and supporting transactions; handling complaints and disputes. Lawful basis: performance of this Agreement. • Complaint materials, including evidence submitted: investigation and fair resolution of Complaints. Lawful basis: performance of this Agreement; establishment or defence of legal claims. • Marketing preferences: sending or withholding marketing communications under clause 7.10. Lawful basis: consent, withdrawable at any time. 7.3 Data collection purpose The Operators collects personal data only where it is adequate, relevant and limited to what is necessary for the stated purposes. Identity evidence is used for verification and compliance, not for marketing or unrelated purposes. Where a purpose can be met with less data, less data is collected. 7.4 Transparency at collection The Privacy Notice, described in clause 7.14, states what is collected, why, on what basis, with whom it is shared, how long it is kept and what rights exist. It is presented to applicants before or at the point of collection and in clear language. 7.5 Retention and secure deletion The Operators retain personal data only as long as necessary for the stated purposes and applicable legal requirements and deletes or anonymises it securely thereafter. Suggested retention periods, to be confirmed with legal advice and stated precisely in the Privacy Notice: verification materials and decisions for a retention period of after the end of the artisan relationship, reflecting the need to respond to disputes and legal claims; order and transaction records for the period required by tax and commercial law; and complaint records for the period needed to resolve them and for any follow-on proceedings. Deletion includes copies held by processors where practicable, subject to their own legal duties. 7.6 Access controls Access to personal data, including verification materials and unpublished designs, is restricted to founders and helpers who need it for a specific purpose. The Operators will maintain an access list recording who can access which categories of data, will revoke access promptly when a person stops helping, and will avoid sharing personal data through channels that do not protect it, such as open group chats. 7.7 Sharing with service providers The Operators may share personal data with hosting providers, WhatsApp (for communications through that channel), Payment Providers (once payments are integrated), and couriers (for delivery), and with professional advisers, and where required by law or to establish or defend legal claims. Each such sharing is limited to what the provider needs, and providers are expected to handle the data lawfully under their own obligations and any data processing agreement between them and the Operators. Before sharing artisan data with any provider, the founders should put a written data processing agreement in place where the provider acts as a processor. 7.8 International transfers Some service providers may process data outside Kenya. Under the Kenyan Data Protection Act, 2019, transfer of personal data outside Kenya is restricted and may take place only where the conditions in the Act are met, including appropriate safeguards, consent of the data subject, or the other grounds recognised under the Act. The Privacy Notice states where processors are located and the basis for any transfer. The founders will confirm, with legal advice and against current guidance of the Data Commissioner, the conditions applying to the specific providers they use. 7.9 Security incidents and breach notification Where a personal data breach is likely to result in a real risk of harm to data subjects, the Operators must notify the Data Commissioner within 72 hours of becoming aware of the breach and must take steps to mitigate the risk; where a breach is likely to cause significant harm, affected data subjects must also be informed. The Operators will maintain a simple incident procedure: detect and contain; assess risk; notify the Data Commissioner where required within 72 hours; inform affected artisans and buyers where required; record the incident and the response. The Operators do not promise perfect security, and clause 8.5 states the practical limits honestly. 7.10 Sensitive personal data The platform does not require artisans to submit sensitive personal data, meaning data revealing health, ethnic origin, religion, or similar categories protected under the Data Protection Act, 2019. If a person chooses to disclose such data in complaints or communications, the Operators will handle it with additional care, restrict access to it, and retain it only as long as necessary. Artisans should avoid submitting sensitive personal data that is not needed for their application or complaint. 7.11 Children and underage applicants The platform is not directed at children. Applicants must be at least 18 years old, and identity confirmation under clause 4.2 serves this limit. Where the Operator learns that a person under 18 has an account, it will suspend the account and delete the personal data collected, subject to any retention legally required. This shall be subject to a valid, voluntary, legal agreement with the child for the sake of handling transaction, distribution, marketing, negotiation of the terms of the transaction. 7.15 No absolute promises The Operators do not claim that the platform collects no personal data; it does, as clause 7.2 states. The Operator does not promise absolute confidentiality or perfect security. SCREENSHOTS, DOWNLOADING AND CONTENT SECURITY 8.1 Contractual rules Buyers and visitors to the platform may view Listings for personal, evaluative and purchasing purposes. ORDERS, DELIVERY, PAYMENTS AND COMMISSIONS 10.1 Direct buyer-to-artisan payment in the pilot During the Pilot Period, the platform does not receive, hold, split or redistribute buyer money. Buyers pay Artisans directly, by the method agreed between them, typically mobile money transfer or other direct payment. The platform's role in payments during the pilot is limited to communicating orders and providing a channel for confirmations and complaints. 10.2 Risks of off-platform payments and limits of oversight Direct payment means the platform does not verify that a payment occurred, cannot recover money on an Artisan's behalf, and cannot guarantee a buyer's or an Artisan's good faith. The parties should follow the safe-practice guidance including: confirming receipt of the exact amount before dispatch; treating screenshots of payment confirmations as unverified claims until the Artisan confirms the money is actually in the account, because fraudulent payment screenshots and fake notifications are known risks; and using platform channels for order records where the parties want a record the Operators can refer to in a complaint. 10.3 Future payment integration If the Operators later integrates payments, it will do so through a Payment Provider authorised under Kenyan law and will announce the change to Artisans before it takes effect, in accordance with clauses 3.6 and 12.6. Integration may change who holds funds at which stage, and this Agreement will state clearly, before the change, whether the Operator receives, holds, splits or redistributes funds. 10.4 Regulatory limits on holding or redistributing funds Under the National Payment System Act, 2011 (Act No. 39 of 2011), section 12, no person may conduct the business of a payment service provider in Kenya except a payment service provider that is authorised under that Act. Conducting the business of a payment service provider includes, in the manner and to the extent defined under that Act and applicable regulations, receiving money from the public for the purpose of payment or transfer services, and can include holding or redistributing funds on behalf of others. Two practical consequences follow: • The platform must not hold, split or redistribute buyer or artisan funds in a way that constitutes payment service business unless and until the Operators, or the provider it uses, is properly authorised for the relevant service. • Access to a payment provider's API does not by itself authorise the platform to operate escrow, split payments or any other fund-holding model. Whether a proposed arrangement is permissible depends on the authorisation held by the Payment Provider and on the structure of the arrangement, and requires separate regulatory review. 10.5 Confirmation, reconciliation and record-keeping For each Order, the Artisan keeps a simple record: the Order details, the amount, the payment date, the payer reference, and the delivery confirmation. The Operators may request an Order summary for complaint handling under clause 11, and the parties should provide accurate records when they do. Payment confirmations shown on the platform are recorded as claims; the underlying verification is the payee's own account confirmation. 10.6 Refunds, cancellations and disputed deliveries A buyer or Artisan may cancel an Order before dispatch in accordance with the Artisan's stated policy. Where payment has been made and an Order is cancelled, cannot be fulfilled, is not delivered, or arrives damaged or materially not as described, the Artisan and buyer should resolve the matter directly in the first instance; the Operators may assist through clause 11 but, during the Pilot Period, does not hold funds and cannot itself issue refunds. Chargebacks or reversals initiated through a payment provider by a buyer are handled by that provider; the Artisan should respond to the provider's process and may seek the Operators' assistance in supplying order records. Where a dispute involves an allegation of fraud, clause 4.11 and clause 11 apply. 10.7 Delivery and couriers Deliveries are made by independent couriers chosen and contracted by the Artisan or the buyer, as agreed between them. The platform does not itself provide courier services. Risk of loss or damage in transit, and responsibility for delays, follow the delivery arrangement the parties agree; the Artisan should state their delivery terms in each Listing, including who chooses and pays the courier, and the process for lost or damaged items. The Operator may publish guidance and may assist in complaints to couriers where a Complaint is filed but is not a party to the carriage contract. 10.8 Commissions and fees No commission applies during the Pilot Period, under clause 3.4. A commission becomes payable only in respect of transactions occurring on or after the disclosed effective date of a commission that was disclosed and agreed in advance. 10.9 Consequences of unpaid commissions Where a lawful, clearly agreed commission model is in force, an Artisan's failure to pay an agreed commission after the agreed process, including invoice, reminder and a reasonable cure period of at least 14 days, may result in graduated measures under clause 12, including restriction of new Listings and, ultimately, termination. COMPLAINTS AND DISPUTE RESOLUTION 11.1 Scope of complaints A Complaint may be submitted about: IP infringement or misuse of a maker's work or identity; impersonation; fraud or suspected fraud; verification decisions; harassment or discrimination; unfair platform treatment; payment administration and payment-related claims; delivery disputes; unauthorised use of personal information; and any other platform-related concern. 11.2 Submission method Complaints are submitted through WhatsApp primarily, in writing where practicable, stating: the complainant and, where applicable, the person complained against; the subject and the facts relied on; the outcome sought; and any supporting evidence. 11.3 Acknowledgment and timelines The Operators will acknowledge receipt within 3 Business Days and will ordinarily complete an initial review within at least 15 Business Days. Complex matters may take longer, and the complainant will be told the expected period and the reason for any extension. 11.4 Evidence submission and secure handling The parties may submit evidence, which the Operators handles as confidential to the extent reasonably practicable, restricts to the reviewers who need it, and retains as described in clause 7.5. Evidence is used for the Complaint and any follow-on proceedings, and for no other purpose, except where disclosure is required by law or necessary to decide the Complaint fairly. 11.5 Opportunity to respond A person complained against receives the substance of the Complaint and a reasonable opportunity to respond before any adverse decision, at least 5 Business Days. Urgent protective measures under clause 11.10 may be applied before a full response where necessary to prevent ongoing harm and are then reviewed promptly. 11.6 Conflicts of interest involving founders Where a founder is the complainant, the person complained against, a witness, the investigator, or the decision-maker in a Complaint, the Operators will, so far as practicable: have the Complaint handled by a founder or helper not involved in the conflict; record the conflict and the handling in the Complaint file; and, where the conflict cannot be adequately managed internally, offer the parties the external options in clause 11.13. Where a founder is proposed as a mediator involving the Operators itself, that founder will not act as mediator for that matter. 11.7 Mediation as the preferred primary method For suitable disputes, including IP disputes, delivery disputes and disputes between artisans, the parties are encouraged to attempt Mediation first. Mediation is facilitated negotiation: a neutral mediator helps the parties communicate and explore settlement; the mediator does not judge the merits or impose a decision; and no party may be forced to settle or to accept any proposed outcome. A party may end the Mediation at any time. Mediation is not legal representation: a mediator does not act as an advocate for either party, or the parties may each take independent legal advice at their own cost. 11.8 Written outcomes with reasons The Operators will communicate the outcome of a Complaint in writing and, where appropriate to the matter, with reasons, including what was found, what measures apply and what review options exist. 11.9 Temporary protective measures Where necessary to prevent ongoing harm while a Complaint is considered, the Operators may apply temporary measures, such as restricting a Listing's visibility under clause 6.11, suspending communications between the parties, or restricting an account. Temporary measures are proportionate, are recorded with reasons, and are reviewed as the Complaint progresses. 11.10 Internal review and escalation A party may request an internal review of a Complaint outcome within at least 10 Business Days of the outcome, stating the grounds. The review is conducted, so far as practicable, by a person not involved in the original decision, and its outcome is given in writing with reasons. 11.11 False or malicious complaints A person who submits a Complaint knowing it to be false, or maliciously and without reasonable basis, may face graduated sanctions under clause 12, and this clause does not limit any legal remedy available to the person complained against. 11.12 Access to external remedies Nothing in this Agreement removes or limits any party's access to courts, regulators, statutory complaint mechanisms or urgent legal remedies. In particular: • A party may at any time complain to a regulator with jurisdiction over the matter concerned, including the Office of the Data Protection Commissioner for data protection matters, the Competition Authority of Kenya for competition matters, the Kenya Copyright Board and KIPI for IP matters, and the National Police Service where criminal conduct is suspected. • Nothing in this Agreement requires a party to complete Mediation or any internal process before exercising a legal right, filing a case, or seeking urgent relief. 11.13 The legal framework for alternative dispute resolution The following statements describe the Kenyan legal framework that supports the processes in this clause: • Article 159(2)(c) of the Constitution of Kenya, 2010 requires courts and tribunals, in exercising judicial authority, to promote alternative forms of dispute resolution, including reconciliation, mediation, arbitration and traditional dispute resolution mechanisms. • Sections 59A to 59D of the Civil Procedure Act (Chapter 21, Laws of Kenya) provide the statutory framework for court-annexed mediation and for reference of suitable matters to alternative dispute resolution. • The Civil Procedure (Court-Annexed Mediation) Rules, 2022 govern court-annexed mediation; under that framework, a settlement agreement arising from mediation may be recorded and, where adopted or registered in accordance with the Rules, is enforceable. • The Small Claims Court Act, 2016 (Act No. 10 of 2016) establishes the Small Claims Court, which has pecuniary jurisdiction of up to one million shillings (KSh 1,000,000) and provides an accessible forum for many buyer-artisan and artisan-artisan disputes within that amount. • The Arbitration Act, 1995 (Act No. 4 of 1995) provides a framework for arbitration as an optional alternative for parties who agree to it; nothing in this Agreement compels arbitration, and clause 12.8 does not require a party to forgo court access. 12.
